# StarBar 1.9.0 — A break-out sleeve, and the right cover for Apple Music songs

StarBar 1.9.0 gives the announcement strip a new cover, a sleeve that stands up out of the strip and tips towards your pointer, and shows the right cover for Apple Music songs that aren't in your library.

**This is the recommended release. Requires macOS 13 or later, and Music or Spotify.**

## A sleeve that breaks out of the strip

- The strip's cover is bigger, turned to face the song's details, with rounded corners and a shadow. It stands above the top of the strip.
- It rises out of the strip as the strip slides up, and sinks back into it as the strip slides away.
- Move the pointer near the strip and the sleeve tips towards it, with a soft shine on the pointer's side.
- It works with every strip style: Classic, Blur and Liquid Glass.
- With Reduce Motion on, the sleeve fades in already in place and doesn't tip. With Reduce Transparency on, it tips without the shine.
- A cover with see-through corners, such as the app icon that Preview shows, is cut to its own shape instead of sitting on a grey square. It stands above the strip on tall screens too.
- To go back to the flat cover inside the strip, run `defaults write com.rossshannon.starbar announcementBreakout -bool false`, then quit StarBar and open it again.

## The right cover for Apple Music songs

- When Music plays a song straight from Apple Music that isn't in your library, it often hands StarBar the wrong cover, such as the previous song's or another album's, or no cover at all. StarBar now asks Apple for the song's own cover and shows it in the strip and the popover.
- The first song of each album shows a grey placeholder for a moment, usually about half a second, and the cover fades in when it arrives. Other songs from the same album show it straight away.
- A few albums can't be found this way, because Apple's search lists only what the iTunes Store sells, and some albums are only on Apple Music. Those show Music's own cover, or the placeholder when Music's is the previous song's.
- Songs in your library, including Apple Music songs you've added, are unchanged: their covers come from your library.

## Network access

StarBar makes one new kind of request. For an Apple Music song that isn't in your library, it sends the song's artist and title, and your Mac's region, which picks the store to search, to Apple's iTunes Search service at `itunes.apple.com`. It then downloads the cover from Apple's image server, `mzstatic.com`. Other songs from the same album use the cover already fetched. The request goes to the company that's already streaming you the song. Songs in your library never cause it.

The Spotify requests are unchanged. The [privacy policy](https://github.com/rossshannon/StarBar/blob/main/Privacy%20Policy.md) is rewritten to list everything StarBar reads, keeps and sends.

## The popover with nothing playing

- With nothing playing, the popover says "Not Playing" and names the song that Play will start, instead of showing an empty cover.
- When Music has no song loaded, as it usually hasn't after you stop it or open it, pressing Play used to do nothing. Now it starts the last song from your library that Music played, and opens Music in the background first if it's closed.
- If StarBar has no song to go back to, or Music can't play it, Play brings Music to the front so you can choose something.
- To do this, StarBar remembers the last library song Music played (its name, artist and Music's ID for it) in its preferences. Each new song replaces it, so it's never a history.

## Smoother strip

- The strip no longer catches for a moment as it slides up the first time after StarBar starts. To get that out of the way, StarBar draws the strip once, invisibly, two seconds after it starts.
- When Music is slow to answer as a song changes, the strip pauses its slide and carries on, rather than jumping to the end and appearing already in place.
- When the pointer opens a see-through hole in the strip, the hole keeps 40% of the background behind the cover and the song's details, so the white text stays readable.

## Preferences

- The rating shortcuts are listed from 5 stars down to Remove stars, best first. Each keeps its own shortcut.

## Installation

Download the zip, extract StarBar and move it to Applications. Allow StarBar to control Music, and Spotify if you use it, when prompted.

This build is signed ad hoc and is not notarised. If macOS blocks it, use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. A SHA-256 checksum accompanies the zip.
