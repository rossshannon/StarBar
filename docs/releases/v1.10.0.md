# StarBar 1.10.0 — Last.fm scrobbling, and a heart that bursts

StarBar 1.10.0 scrobbles to Last.fm, makes the favourite heart burst when you set it, can tint the Music Video strip from the album art, and puts Preferences in tabs.

**This is the recommended release. Requires macOS 13 or later, and Music or Spotify.**

## Scrobbling to Last.fm

- StarBar can now scrobble to Last.fm. Press Connect next to Last.fm in Preferences and allow access on Last.fm's page.
- From then on, each song you play in Music is added to your Last.fm profile once it has played for half its length or four minutes, and a heart you set or clear in StarBar loves or unloves the song there.
- Spotify songs are scrobbled only if you tick "Scrobble songs played in Spotify". It's off by default, because Last.fm's own Spotify link already scrobbles them, and both together would count every song twice.
- Nothing is sent to Last.fm until you connect, and the privacy policy lists what is.

## A heart that bursts

- Click the heart to make a song a favourite and it bursts: the outline shrinks away, a ring of colour swells and opens, and the filled heart pops through it while seven pairs of coloured dots fly out.
- In the menu bar the dots fly down and to the sides, below the menu bar, so nothing is cut off at the top of the screen. The burst never gets in the way: a second click goes straight through to the heart, even mid-burst.
- If the Music Video strip is up, its heart bursts too, with its dots flying up over the strip, and the strip waits for the burst to finish before it slides away.
- Spotify's green heart bursts in greens when you like a song.
- Taking a heart away is quieter: it dips and comes back as an outline.
- Only your own clicks set it off. A favourite changed in Music, or a new song that's already a favourite, shows its heart without a burst.
- With Reduce Motion on, the heart cross-fades instead.

## Album art tinting on the Music Video strip

- A new Album art tinting setting in Preferences colours the strip from the song's cover: tint the whole strip, or glow from behind the cover as if it were a light. It's off until you choose one.
- The colour is the cover's most vivid one, kept dark enough that the white text stays easy to read. A black-and-white cover leaves the strip as it was.
- When the song changes while the strip is up, or the cover arrives late, the colour fades across.

## Preferences in tabs

- Preferences has tabs, like the Mac's own Settings windows: General for how StarBar behaves and the Music Video strip, Accounts for Spotify and Last.fm, and Shortcuts for all ten keyboard shortcuts, which now have a page of their own.
- The window opens on the tab you used last.

## Your own copy, when Apple Music names it differently

- When Music plays a song from the Apple Music catalog that you already have under a slightly different title or on another release, StarBar now finds your copy and shows its stars and heart, rather than offering to add the song again. "I Wanna Dance with Somebody (Who Loves Me)" finds your "I Wanna Dance With Somebody", and a song played from an anniversary edition finds your copy on the original album.
- When your copy is on another album, the strip and the player show the cover of the album that's playing.

## Smoother stars

- When a song starts, the stars roll into the menu bar without catching halfway. Just after a song starts, Music often announces it a second time and reports a library change, and StarBar's reads of Music for each could hold the stars still for a quarter of a second. StarBar now waits for the stars to finish rolling before it reads Music again.
- When your Mac is busy, menu bar animations pause and carry on where they were, rather than jumping ahead.
- When the stars take over from Spotify's heart, the first star no longer shows over the menu bar icon to the right of StarBar.
- On macOS 26 and earlier, a quick drag across the stars could save one star fewer than the one you let go on. It now saves the star you let go on.

## Installation

Download the zip, extract StarBar and move it to Applications. Allow StarBar to control Music, and Spotify if you use it, when prompted.

This build is signed ad hoc and is not notarised. If macOS blocks it, use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. A SHA-256 checksum accompanies the zip.
